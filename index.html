Blackjack Basic Strategy

What is basic strategy?

For every hand, one play loses the least money over time. That play depends only on your cards and the dealer's one face-up card. Basic strategy is the list of those plays, worked out by math. Follow it every time and the casino's edge drops to about 0.5%. You'll still lose some hands. You just stop making mistakes.

Card values

Number cards count as their number. J, Q and K count as 10. An Ace counts as 11, unless that would take you over 21, then it counts as 1.

  • Hard hand: no Ace, or the Ace has to count as 1. 10 + 6 = hard 16. A + 6 + 10 = hard 17.
  • Soft hand: an Ace counting as 11. One more card can't bust you, because the Ace can drop to 1. A + 6 = soft 17.
  • Pair: your first two cards are the same, like 8 + 8. Any two 10-value cards (10, J, Q, K) are a pair of 10s, which you never split anyway.

Your four choices

  • Hit Take another card. You can keep hitting.
  • Stand Take no more cards.
  • Double Double your bet and get exactly one more card. Only allowed on your first two cards.
  • Split Only with a pair. Put out a second bet and play each card as its own hand.

Can't double? (for example, you already have three cards) The chart tells you what to do instead: D means hit, Ds means stand.

How to look up any hand

  1. A pair? Use the Pairs chart.
  2. An Ace counting as 11? Use the Soft chart.
  3. Anything else: use the Hard chart.
  4. Go across to the column for the dealer's face-up card.

This works after you hit too. A, 2, 4 is soft 17, so read the soft 17 row.

The big idea

The dealer has no choices: they must hit until they reach 17 or more. That's why their face-up card matters so much.

  • Dealer shows 2–6 (weak, busts often): stand on hard 13–16 and let them bust. Double more often.
  • Dealer shows 7–A (strong): hit hard 12–16. Standing loses too often.
  • Always stand on hard 17 or more.
  • Always split Aces and 8s. Never split 5s or 10s.
  • Never take insurance or "even money". It's a bad side bet, so it's left out of the chart.

Learn it in 4 steps

Study one chart, then drill just that type of hand until you're getting 90% or more right. Then move on.

          A simplified 5-rule cheat sheet that captures ~90% of full basic strategy's value with far less to memorize. Source: Kevin Stone. These 5 rules are a standalone quick-start, not a supplement to the full chart (Rule 5 uses surrender, which the rest of this site doesn't assume).

          Use this until you've memorized the full chart. It's simplified, so a few plays differ from the Chart tab. Once you know the chart, go with the chart.

          Quick glossary: "hard" total = no ace, or an ace counting as 1 (e.g. 10+6 = hard 16). "soft" total = a hand with an ace counting as 11 (e.g. A+7 = soft 18). Dealer card = the one face-up card you can see.

          1. Stand
            Your hand totals 12–16 (hard), and dealer shows 2–6 e.g. you have 10+5 = 16, dealer shows a 4 → stay put, the dealer is likely to bust.
          2. Double
            Your hand totals 9, 10, or 11 (hard), and dealer shows 2–8 e.g. you have 6+4 = 10, dealer shows a 7 → double your bet, take exactly one more card.
          3. Split
            You're dealt a pair of 2s, 3s, 6s, 7s, 8s, or Aces — any dealer card e.g. you have 8+8, no matter what the dealer shows → split into two separate hands.
          4. Double
            Your hand is soft 13–18 (an ace + a 2–7), and dealer shows 4, 5, or 6 e.g. you have A+6 = soft 17, dealer shows a 5 → double your bet, take one more card.
          5. Surrender
            Your hand totals 15 or 16 (hard), and dealer shows a 10-value card or Ace e.g. you have 10+6 = 16, dealer shows a King → give up half your bet and end the hand, if your casino allows it.

          Anything not covered above: stand on hard 17+, soft 19+, and soft 18 when the dealer shows 2–8 (never split 10s); otherwise hit.
